This structure ensures the evolution engine reinforces ethical alignment rather than being captured by corporate consolidation.

## Section 5: Dispute Resolution & Charter Amendment

### 5.1 Trustee-Visionary Disagreement on Licensing

If Trustees recommend licensing a third party and Visionary disagrees:

1. **Written Dispute Submission:** Trustees provide written recommendation with financial analysis, strategic rationale, and proposed license terms
2. **Visionary Review Period:** Visionary has 30 days to respond with approval, rejection, or modification
3. **If Visionary Declines:** Decision is final; no appeal; Trustees execute Visionary's decision
4. **If Visionary Proposes Modifications:** Trustees accept, modify the agreement, and re-submit for final approval
5. **If Trustees Believe Visionary's Rejection is "Materially Contrary to Trust Interests":**
   - Trustees may escalate to neutral arbitration (Israeli arbitration board, accredited by Israel Bar Association)
   - Trustees must prove by clear evidence that licensing is "in the material best interest of the Trust and Visionary"
   - **Burden on Trustees:** They must overcome Visionary's preference (high threshold)
   - **Default:** Visionary's decision prevails unless Trustees meet the burden

### 5.2 Charter Amendments (High Barrier)

Amendments to this Trust charter require:

- **Written request** from Visionary (or Trustees proposing amendment on behalf of Visionary)
- **Unanimous approval** from all three Trustees
- **Visionary sign-off** (cannot amend over Visionary's objection)

**Amendments Cannot:**
- Strip the Visionary's Exclusive Use Right (Section 2.1)
- Remove the Visionary's Rollout Authority (Section 2.2)
- Weaken the Ethical Licensing Shield (Section 2.3)
- Extend Trustee terms beyond 14 years without Visionary consent
- Transfer Trust assets outside the named beneficiary lineage
- Sunset the Trust before 100 years have elapsed

**Permitted Amendments:**
- Update Trustee compensation
- Adjust royalty rates for specific license tiers
- Add successor designations or name contingent heirs
- Clarify implementation details of rollout triggers

### 5.3 Succession Upon Visionary's Death

**All Retained Rights (exclusive use + rollout authority) transfer automatically to the designated Primary Heir upon Visionary's death.**

- **Automatic Transfer:** No court probate, no delay, no contestation; rights pass by operation of Trust law
- **Heir Authority:** Primary Heir steps into all Visionary roles immediately (can veto licenses, inspect records, remove Trustees, amend charter)
- **Trustee Continuity:** Existing Trustees remain in office during transition (no automatic replacement)
- **Notifications:** Trustees and Vision 100 Foundation must be notified within 30 days
- **Trustee Duty to Heir:** All three Trustees owe same fiduciary duties to new heir as they did to original Visionary

**Succession Order (If Primary Heir Predeceases):**
1. Primary Heir (Visionary-designated) → All rights
2. Secondary Heir (Visionary-designated) → All rights if Primary predeceases
3. Tertiary Heir (Visionary-designated) → All rights if Primary and Secondary predecease
4. **Failure to Designate Heirs:** If Visionary dies without designating heirs, patent rights vest in Vision 100 Foundation (Foundation becomes trustee and licensee; public interest override)

## Section 8: The 100-Year Covenant & Perpetual Sovereignty

### 8.1 Charter Binding Period: 100 Years

**This Trust charter is irrevocable and binding for 100 years, from June 3, 2026 to June 3, 2126.**

During this period:
- Trustees cannot dissolve the Trust
- Patents cannot be forced to auction or liquidation
- Visionary's Retained Rights cannot be stripped or diminished
- No external party (government, creditor, court) can seize the patents for reasons other than criminal infringement liability

### 8.2 Transition Options at the 100-Year Mark (June 3, 2126)

At the end of the 100-year period, the Visionary's **designated heir (4th generation+)** chooses one of three paths:

**Option 1: Renew for Another 100 Years**
- Heir executes renewal document with unanimous Trustee approval
- Trust continues under same charter; Retained Rights remain intact
- Creates indefinite, perpetual control option for subsequent generations

**Option 2: Transition to Public Domain**
- Heir authorizes Trustees to release all patents into public domain (GPL 3.0 or Creative Commons license)
- Patents become freely available to global community
- Trade secrets remain confidential (not released); only patents transition
- Trustees file public domain declarations with patent offices worldwide

**Option 3: Perpetual Private Trade Secrets**
- Heir elects to maintain confidentiality indefinitely
- Patents are allowed to expire (not renewed); Trade secrets remain private
- Layer 14 methodology lives on as proprietary competitive advantage, protected only by secrecy
- Useful if synthetic biology or digital consciousness has made distillation obsolete (or its value is so high that maintaining secrecy is more valuable than patents)

### 8.3 Purpose: The Sovereignty Guarantee

**The 100-year term is the cornerstone of the structural moat.**

By the time the 100-year covenant expires:
- The original Visionary is deceased (great-great-grandchildren era)
- Corporate consolidation, hostile M&A, and government seizure threats have evolved unpredictably
- The heir's decision (renew, release, privatize) reflects *their* vision for humanity, not corporate pressure

This structure ensures:
1. **No forced licensing during the Visionary's lifetime** — Evolution engine remains exclusive
2. **Ancestor's intent protected for 4+ generations** — Descendants inherit control, not debt
3. **Option to share at scale** — Renewal OR release; heir chooses based on 2126 context
4. **Perpetual alternative** — If releasing seems unwise, keeping as trade secret is always available

**Ultimate effect:** Layer 14 cannot be captured by corporate consolidation, hostile takeovers, or government seizure during the Visionary's lifetime and direct lineage. The Visionary's great-great-grandchildren (or chosen successors) decide the fate of the evolution engine at the century mark.
