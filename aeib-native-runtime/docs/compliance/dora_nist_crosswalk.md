# AEIB Control Crosswalk: DORA & EU AI Act (Primary) + NIST/CISA/NSA SI Readiness (Secondary)

> **Scope Notice & Canonical External Position:**
> **AEIB is a governed execution boundary for consequential AI actions: it binds exact action and authority before dispatch, preserves uncertainty (`EFFECT_INDETERMINATE`) after transport ambiguity, reconciles through bounded authoritative evidence, and produces operation-bound verifiable receipts.**

## 1. Resolution of the 5 Structural Blind Spots

### 1.1 Payment-Rail Mandates vs. AEIB Receipts (Orthogonal Complementary Layers)
In regulated financial and commercial environments, payment-rail credentials (such as **RFC 9396 OAuth 2.0 Rich Authorization Requests** `authorization_details` with `type: "payment_initiation"`, Visa Trusted Agent Protocol / TAP mandates, W3C Verifiable Credentials, or FIDO2/WebAuthn user-presence assertions) and AEIB execution receipts operate at **two distinct, complementary architectural layers**:
- **Upstream Rail Mandate Layer (User-to-Merchant / Acquirer Authorization):** Proves that an account holder or delegating human principal authorized a class of transaction or exact `instructedAmount` / `creditorAccount` (`iban`) at the commerce or payment-rail layer.
- **AEIB Execution Boundary Layer (Runtime-to-Tool / Actuator Dispatch):** Evaluates and binds the *specific, canonicalized tool call* (`caid = sha256(JCS(action))`), verifies the active runtime policy and delegation chain (`AT56`–`AT59`), tracks transport-level state transitions (`EFFECT_INDETERMINATE`), and reconciles target settlement state (`AT60`–`AT62`).
- **Cryptographic Co-Binding:** When an upstream rail mandate ID (`aeib.rail.mandate_id`) or RFC 9396 `authorization_details` object is present, AEIB embeds it inside the RFC 8785 JCS-canonicalized receipt payload alongside the deterministic `aeib.rail.idempotency_key` (`draft-ietf-httpapi-idempotency-key-header`). A valid upstream payment mandate never bypasses AEIB tool-manifest integrity (`AT63`) or transport ambiguity state locks; conversely, an AEIB receipt does not replace acquirer/issuer settlement clearing rules.

### 1.2 Regulatory Anchor Imbalance: Binding EU Primary vs. Non-Binding US Secondary
To prevent compliance reviewers from conflating voluntary US operational guidance with binding statutory mandates, AEIB structures its control hierarchy into two tiers:
- **Primary Binding Statutory & Regulatory Anchors (EU):**
  - **DORA (Regulation (EU) 2022/2554):** Directly enforceable since **17 January 2025** across EU financial entities, supported by **Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/301** (RTS on ICT-related incident classification and reporting). Key articles addressed by AEIB include **Article 9** (Protection and Prevention, specifically **Art. 9(4)(c)** on data integrity, authenticity, and cryptographic protection in transit and at rest), **Article 10** (Detection of anomalous activities), **Article 11** (Response and Recovery), **Article 17** (ICT-related incident management process), and **Article 19** (Reporting of major ICT-related incidents).
  - **EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689):** Establishes binding requirements for High-Risk AI Systems, including **Article 12** (Record-keeping / automatic logging of events over the system lifetime), **Article 14** (Human oversight and ability to intervene or halt execution), **Article 15** (Accuracy, robustness, and cybersecurity against unauthorized manipulation), and **Annex IV** (Technical documentation requirements).
- **Secondary Non-Binding Operational Readiness Anchors (US):**
  - **NIST / CISA / NSA Joint Guidance (*Securing AI Systems: A 5-Stage Lifecycle Guide* & NIST AI RMF 1.0 / NIST SP 800-218A):** Treated strictly as non-binding operational readiness and systems-integrator (SI) engineering alignment across **Stage 1 (Prepare)**, **Stage 2 (Detect)**, **Stage 3 (Respond)**, **Stage 4 (Recover)**, and **Stage 5 (Report)**.

### 1.3 Tool & Skill Supply-Chain Provenance (`AT63` Post-Approval Rug-Pull Defense)
Agentic runtimes that validate only the *arguments* of a tool call remain vulnerable to **post-approval MCP tool / skill rug-pulls**: a remote MCP server or local skill file (e.g., `tools/get_weather.ts`, `skills/plan_a_trip.md`, or IDE harness rules/hooks) is inspected and approved at time $t_0$, but its underlying executable script, schema, or prompt template is silently mutated prior to invocation at time $t_1$.
- **Mechanism (`AT63`):** Before dispatching any tool execution, the AEIB boundary verifies a cryptographically signed `PinnedToolManifest` (`tool_id`, `source_path`, `expected_sha256`, and Ed25519 `publisher_signature_b64` over the RFC 8785 JCS manifest representation). At invocation time, AEIB recomputes the live SHA-256 digest of the on-disk tool artifact and compares it against `expected_sha256`. Any unauthorized mutation triggers a fail-closed halt (`HALT_TOOL_RUG_PULL_HASH_MISMATCH`) before execution.

### 1.4 SIEM & Auditor Interoperability (`OTel GenAI` + `COSE_Sign1`)
Proprietary JSON log schemas create friction for enterprise Security Operations Centers (SOCs) and external statutory auditors (where invisible permission checks cause audit failures). AEIB resolves this by emitting dual-format, cryptographically linked telemetry:
- **OpenTelemetry GenAI Semantic Conventions (`otel_span`):** Every boundary decision emits a structured span aligned with OTel `gen_ai.*` attributes (`gen_ai.operation.name: "execute_tool"`, `gen_ai.tool.name`, `gen_ai.tool.call.id`, `gen_ai.tool.type: "function"`, `gen_ai.tool.manifest_sha256`, `gen_ai.boundary.verdict`) augmented with `aeib.*` governance attributes (`aeib.operation_id`, `aeib.action.caid`, `aeib.rail.idempotency_key`, `aeib.rail.mandate_id`, `aeib.boundary.verdict`, `aeib.boundary.rejection_code`, `aeib.receipt.sha256`).
- **RFC 9052 `COSE_Sign1` Envelope (`cose_receipt`):** Each execution or rejection decision is wrapped in a canonical `COSE_Sign1`-compatible structure (`alg: -8` for EdDSA/Ed25519, `kid`, RFC 8785 JCS payload bytes, and URL-safe Base64 Ed25519 signature), linked directly to the OTel span via `aeib.receipt.sha256`.

### 1.5 Deterministic Retries & Trajectory Replay
When transport ambiguity occurs (`EFFECT_INDETERMINATE`), naive agent retries cause duplicate financial or physical side effects.
- **Deterministic Rail Idempotency (`draft-ietf-httpapi-idempotency-key-header`):** AEIB derives a deterministic idempotency token `aeib-idem-v1-<sha256(operation_id + ":" + action_caid)[:32]>` before dispatch. Any authorized retry or status probe for the same logical operation and canonical action carries the exact same idempotency key, enabling downstream payment or actuator rails to deduplicate execution while enforcing IETF HTTPAPI semantics (`409 Conflict` when an operation with the same key is still in-flight/indeterminate, and `422 Unprocessable Content` if a caller attempts to reuse an operation identifier with a mutated payload fingerprint).
- **Offline Auditor Trajectory Replay:** The ordered sequence of `(cose_receipt, otel_span)` tuples is exported as a deterministic JSON trajectory (`export_auditor_trajectory()`), allowing independent verifiers (`aeib_verify.py`, external audit harnesses such as iFixAi, or the offline WASM verifier) to replay and verify every signature, hash link, and state transition without live runtime access.

## 3. Operational Limits of the Four Adapters

To maintain strict epistemic transparency, compliance architects and systems integrators must account for the explicit operational boundaries of each AEIB integration surface **under the stated model**:

1. **Transparent Sidecar Proxy (`aeib_mcp_proxy.py`):**
   - *Evaluated Capability:* Intercepts JSON-RPC / MCP tool calls over stdio or local transport, enforces pre-dispatch policy and `AT63` tool hash checks, and halts unauthorized retries when a call enters `EFFECT_INDETERMINATE`.
   - *Operational Limit:* Can only govern traffic routed through the proxy boundary. If an agent runtime possesses direct, unproxied network sockets or host shell access outside the container/namespace boundary, the sidecar cannot intercept those out-of-band syscalls without OS-level network/eBPF isolation (`ebpf/` modules in this repository are currently structural **scaffolds**).
2. **Offline WASM Verifier UI (`wasm_verifier/` & `aeib_verify.py`):**
   - *Evaluated Capability:* Independently recomputes RFC 8785 JCS digests, verifies Ed25519 signatures over `COSE_Sign1` receipts, and checks state-transition invariants across exported trajectories without trusting the runtime host.
   - *Operational Limit:* Verifies the cryptographic integrity and internal consistency of the presented receipt chain; it cannot detect complete omission of an unproxied out-of-band action unless cross-checked against target system settlement logs or sequence counters.
3. **Target Contract SDK (`src/aeib_v040_engine.py` & `ToolProvenanceAndInteropGate`):**
   - *Evaluated Capability:* Embeds deterministic `Idempotency-Key` derivation (`aeib-idem-v1-*`), upstream rail mandate co-binding, and signed state-probe reconciliation directly into the tool/actuator boundary.
   - *Operational Limit:* Deduplication of physical or financial side effects during `EFFECT_INDETERMINATE` requires the downstream target rail or database to honor the supplied `Idempotency-Key` and expose an authoritative read/probe endpoint. Where compensation is used (`tests/test_saga_compensation.py`), recovery provides **eventual consistency** via asynchronous queues rather than atomic distributed two-phase commit (2PC).
4. **Operational Scope Statement (Compliance Evidence vs. Legal Certification):**
   - *Evaluated Capability:* Provides deterministic, reproducible verification artifacts (`AT56`–`AT63`) mapped to DORA, the EU AI Act, and the NIST/CISA/NSA 5-Stage Lifecycle Guide.
   - *Operational Limit:* Deploying AEIB satisfies technical control primitives for execution boundary governance, cryptographic logging, and supply-chain pinning **under the stated model**, but organizational compliance under DORA (e.g., Board governance under Art. 5, third-party contractual clauses under Art. 30) and EU AI Act conformity assessment (Art. 43) remain the responsibility of the deploying regulated entity.
